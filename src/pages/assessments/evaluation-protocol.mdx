# Evaluation Protocol

This page is the single canonical source for the evaluation and marking
mechanics shared by all three projects. Each project page carries a concise,
project-specific summary and links back here for the complete rules.

## Public evaluation layers

Each project supplies two public evaluation layers. They serve different
purposes and must remain separate from training data.

1. **Five development examples with ground truth.** Each example contains a
   prompt and its reference continuation or answer. Use these examples to
   verify that the evaluation pipeline, masking, tokenizer, and sampler work
   end to end. They may also appear as clearly labelled qualitative cases in
   the report. They are diagnostic examples, not the source of the official
   submitted-model mark.
2. **Ten tutor-evaluation prompts.** The ten prompts are public before the
   deadline, but their reference continuations or answers are withheld. Each
   item is stored as one string: a truncated source-story prefix for
   Projects 1 and 2, the relevant narrative input for Project 3 Track A, or
   the task input for Project 3 Track B. The teaching team runs the frozen
   submission against all ten items.

Being public is not being permitted for training use: releasing these prompts
is **not permission to train or tune against them**. Both layers are subject
to the prohibited uses below.

## Prohibited uses

Released prompts, development ground truth, withheld tutor ground truth, and
clean reserve cases must not be used for:

- training;
- validation;
- retrieval;
- tokenizer construction;
- filtering;
- prompt-template design;
- synthetic-data seeding;
- sampler tuning;
- checkpoint selection; or
- manual output editing.

Students must not reconstruct source continuations, search for withheld
answers, or attempt to obtain private material from staff, peers, repository
history, network requests, or the marking harness.

## Separation validation

Before training, students must run the repository's supplied **exact- and
near-duplicate separation validator** over their proposed corpus and retain
its report in the experiment record. The validator checks exact matches and
likely near-duplicate overlap against the released evaluation prompts.

Passing the validator is evidence of due diligence, not permission to ignore
provenance and not a guarantee that no contamination exists. If a student
discovers likely overlap, or encounters material they believe belongs to the
withheld or reserve set, they must stop using it and email
`llm-training@slopu.edu.au`; see [Policies §3](/policies/#3-evaluation-set-contamination)
for the full contamination-disclosure process.

## Perplexity calculation

Official perplexity is calculated on the withheld continuation or answer
only: prompt tokens provide context but do not contribute to scored negative
log-likelihood. Losses are pooled across all ten tutor items before
exponentiation. To keep the fixed thresholds meaningful when a student
changes tokenizer, the course reports **reference-token-normalised
perplexity**:

<div class="math-block" style="display:block;margin:1rem 0;padding:0.85rem 1.1rem;border-radius:0.5rem;background:var(--at-bg-alt);font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,monospace;overflow-x:auto;">
  P&nbsp;=&nbsp;exp( ( Σ<sub>i</sub> NLL<sub>i</sub> ) / ( Σ<sub>i</sub> N<sub>i</sub><sup>ref</sup> ) )
</div>

where NLL<sub>i</sub> is the submitted model's total negative log-likelihood
for the ground-truth continuation or answer of tutor-evaluation item *i*, and
N<sub>i</sub><sup>ref</sup> is that same raw target's token count under the
published course reference tokenizer. With the default tokenizer, this is
ordinary continuation-only perplexity. Students who change tokenizer should
also report **BPB** (bits-per-byte) as a diagnostic, but the official
thresholds below still use P.

For a project whose submitted-model component is worth *M* marks, two thirds
of that component is the perplexity score:

<div class="math-block" style="display:block;margin:1rem 0;padding:0.85rem 1.1rem;border-radius:0.5rem;background:var(--at-bg-alt);font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,monospace;overflow-x:auto;">
  S<sub>PPL</sub>&nbsp;=&nbsp;(2M/3) ×
  <span style="display:inline-flex;flex-direction:column;vertical-align:middle;margin-left:0.4rem;padding-left:0.75rem;border-left:2px solid currentColor;">
    <span style="white-space:nowrap;">1, &nbsp;if P ≤ 25</span>
    <span style="white-space:nowrap;">exp[−0.1 × (P − 25)], &nbsp;if 25 &lt; P &lt; 50</span>
    <span style="white-space:nowrap;">0, &nbsp;if P ≥ 50</span>
  </span>
</div>

- PPL contributes **two thirds** of the submitted-model component;
  task-specific performance contributes the remaining third.
- P ≤ 25 earns all PPL marks, **not all submitted-model marks** — low
  perplexity cannot substitute for the required behaviour.
- Model marks are calculated at full precision and rounded only when the
  project total is recorded.
- For orientation, P = 35 retains about 36.8% of the available PPL marks and
  P = 45 retains about 13.5%.

## Human evaluation

The remaining one third of each submitted-model component is calculated from
a pre-declared task-specific review. The teaching team generates one output
for every tutor-evaluation prompt using the **frozen checkpoint**, the
**submitted sampler configuration**, and the published seed schedule.
Students do not select, regenerate, or edit these outputs.

Story-based outputs are de-identified — student, model, and sample
identifiers are all removed — and independently reviewed by **three trained
blind raters**. Raters see the prompt, output, task definition, and rating
anchors, but not the student's identity, report, architecture, method, or
existing marks. Output order is randomised whenever a comparison output is
shown.

If the highest and lowest ratings for an item differ by **three or more
points** on a dimension, a **fourth blind rater** adjudicates that dimension;
the final item score is the **median of all valid ratings**.

### Shared rating anchors (0–4)

| Rating | Anchor |
| ---: | --- |
| **4 — Strong** | The behaviour is clear and sustained; the output remains usable, coherent, and free from a material failure on this dimension. |
| **3 — Effective** | The behaviour is present and generally reliable, with a minor lapse that does not undermine the output as a whole. |
| **2 — Mixed** | Some relevant behaviour is present, but it is inconsistent, weak, or offset by a noticeable failure. |
| **1 — Weak** | Only limited evidence of the behaviour is visible, or a major failure makes most of the output unusable. |
| **0 — Absent/invalid** | The behaviour is absent, the output is empty or malformed, or failure on this dimension prevents meaningful assessment. |

For a submitted-model component worth *M* marks, the task-specific score is:

<div class="math-block" style="display:block;margin:1rem 0;padding:0.85rem 1.1rem;border-radius:0.5rem;background:var(--at-bg-alt);font-family:ui-monospace,Menlo,monospace;overflow-x:auto;">
  S<sub>task</sub>&nbsp;=&nbsp;(M/3) × Σ<sub>d</sub> w<sub>d</sub> ( r̄<sub>d</sub> / 4 ),&nbsp;&nbsp; Σ<sub>d</sub> w<sub>d</sub> = 1
</div>

where w<sub>d</sub> is the published dimension weight and r̄<sub>d</sub> is its
mean 0–4 score across the ten tutor prompts. A dimension measured by an exact
verifier or success rate q<sub>d</sub> is converted as r̄<sub>d</sub> = 4 q<sub>d</sub>.
Human preference cannot override an incorrect arithmetic answer or an invalid
required format. Aggregated dimension results and the number of adjudicated
items are returned with feedback.

### Task-specific dimensions and weights

| Project/track | Task-specific dimensions and weights |
| --- | --- |
| **Project 1** | Connection to the supplied prefix **35%**; narrative coherence and fluency **30%**; avoidance of repetition or degeneration **20%**; stopping and completeness **15%**. |
| **Project 2** | Recognisable target behaviour **35%**; narrative coherence and usability **30%**; robustness across varied openings **20%**; retention and integrity, including unacceptable regression or copying **15%**. Raters compare de-identified outputs from the submitted and unchanged starting checkpoints where retention is relevant. |
| **Project 3 Track A** | Objective satisfaction of the declared condition **40%**; narrative quality **25%**; held-out generalisation and prompt robustness **20%**; retention of the monitored starting capability **15%**. Automatically checkable conditions are scored by the published verifier. |
| **Project 3 Track B** | Exact final-answer accuracy **55%**; required output-format validity **15%**; accuracy on unseen values and templates within the published scope **20%**; retained language and instruction reliability **10%**. The first three dimensions are computed by the evaluation harness; three blind raters assess only the final qualitative dimension. |

Automatically verified dimensions cannot be overridden by human preference.
The review is criterion-referenced rather than comparative: students are not
ranked against one another, and Project 3 tracks are not placed on one
leaderboard. Raters do not reward stylistic preference, method complexity, or
resemblance to one withheld reference answer.

## Technical gates

The teaching team evaluates the frozen Hugging Face revision submitted before
the deadline. The submitted package must load using the supplied command and
must reproduce the declared model configuration, tokenizer, sampler, and
parameter count.

- A packaging error may be corrected only when the teaching team can verify
  that the weights and reported results have not changed.
- A checkpoint that cannot be evaluated after the permitted packaging
  correction receives **zero for the submitted-model component**; the report
  is still marked on the evidence it contains.
- A model above the **33.6M** hard boundary, including the published 5%
  tolerance over the 32M design target, is **not eligible for submitted-model
  marks**.
- Evaluation contamination, undeclared external weights, fabricated
  evidence, or deliberate compute-accounting circumvention is handled under
  **academic-integrity procedures**, not as a normal performance deduction.
- Models are not ranked against classmates. Tutor-evaluation metrics and
  blinded human judgements are interpreted against the published task
  criteria.
